## 1. AI agents

TL;DR: OS-level agents were blocked by Signal as an existential privacy threat, autonomous hacking agents found a MONAI Label zero-day in about 11 minutes, and Anthropic closed its IPO this week.

Top stories of the week:

- Signal disclosed on Nov 20 that it is restricting the AI agents built into mobile operating systems - Gemini Intelligence on Android and Onscreen Awareness in Siri on iOS 27 - from capturing Signal chats, because on-device helpers scan what appears on screen. Signal's president Meredith Whittaker called OS-level agentic AI an existential threat to privacy apps and admits the screenshot-blocking fix is incomplete, since agents can also pull data through the filesystem ([Signal blog](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/), [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/signal-blocks-apple-google-ai-screenshots/), [AIBriefs, Nov 22](https://aibriefs.news/ai-news-today)).
- Amazon's threat intelligence team says autonomous Cairn hacking agents, linked to China, found a zero-day in MONAI Label, a medical-imaging annotation tool, going from mapping the API to writing files on a university server in about 11 minutes and planting a Python reverse shell for root. The path-traversal bug affects MONAI Label 0.8.5 and earlier, where authentication is off by default; a fix is merged but no patched release exists yet ([AIBriefs, Nov 22](https://aibriefs.news/ai-news-today)).
- METR published an updated time-horizon study: the task length frontier models can complete keeps doubling, and models match or beat skilled humans on coding and optimization tasks while lagging on open-ended judgment tasks ([AIBriefs, Nov 22](https://aibriefs.news/ai-news-today)).
- AWS made two agent-infrastructure moves on Nov 21: AWS Quick now supports plugins bundling agents, skills, connectors and flows, and Web Search on Amazon Bedrock and AgentCore gained new language and country-aware search options ([Changes.Watch changelog, Nov 21](https://www.changes.watch/updates/daily/2026-11-21)).
- Anthropic closed its Nasdaq IPO this week (labeled Nov 16-22) at roughly $93.5 billion gross plus about $26 billion in a private placement to AMD, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and others, trading as ANT since Nov 19 ([AI World Today, Nov 22](https://aiworldtoday.com/news/weekly-ai-news-anthropic-ipo-november-22-2026)).

Trend read of the week: agents are moving off the chat window and into the operating system, the filesystem and the exploit chain. The week's three signals (Signal's OS-agent blocking, autonomous zero-day discovery, METR's coding-task parity) all point the same way: the security perimeter of agentic AI is the device and the API surface, not the prompt.

Money angles of the week:

- Build a small open-source Python tool that scans a repo's API for default-off authentication and path-traversal exposure before an agent harness touches it, with the MONAI Label case as the README case study. This demonstrates the AI-agents-plus-security intersection to Helsinki-area employers like WithSecure, whose junior/associate roles are realistic for this profile.
- Publish one of the first public walkthroughs of AWS Quick's new plugin format plus a working example plugin to github.com/vilppolahti, mirrored as a YouTube video. AWS consultancies in Espoo (Eficode, Futurice, Gofore) bill for exactly this Bedrock/agent glue work, and being early is a credible interview conversation starter.

## 2. Defence technology

TL;DR: Ukrainian naval drones struck the Russian military cargo ship Sparta II in the Gulf of Finland, and Nato's Baltic Sentry is hardening into a permanent AUV-based maritime mission.

Top stories of the week:

- Drones struck the Sparta II, a US-sanctioned Russian cargo ship operated under the Russian Defence Ministry, on Saturday morning Nov 21 in the eastern Gulf of Finland while it sailed from St Petersburg to Kaliningrad. The ship lost steering, drifted and burned for over a day. Finland's Defence Forces said Ukraine was probably behind the strike, noting Ukraine had claimed a drone hit on the Russian corvette Naro-Fominsk near Gogland island only days earlier ([Al Jazeera, Nov 21](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/21/russia-says-kaliningrad-bound-ship-struck-by-drones-in-gulf-of-finland), [DroneXL, Nov 22](https://dronexl.co/2026/11/22/sparta-ii-russian-military-cargo-ship-drone-strike-gulf-of-finland/), [Defence Matters, Nov 21](https://defencematters.eu/ukrainian-drone-strike-sparta-ii-finland-baltic-sea/)).
- Nato's Baltic Sentry activity, set up in January 2025 to guard Baltic undersea cables and pipelines, is hardening into a permanent maritime mission built around autonomous underwater vehicles: German and Finnish naval units published footage of Seafox remotely operated vehicles and HUGIN 1000 AUVs inspecting seabed infrastructure ([Defence Matters, Nov 21](https://defencematters.eu/nato-underwater-robots-baltic-sea-cable-security/)).
- In US procurement, the Air Force issued a roughly $16 billion engine-maintenance contract spread across dozens of companies, reported Nov 21 ([ArtificialWeapons daily brief](https://artificialweapons.com/daily/2026-11-21/)).

Trend read of the week: the war reached Finnish-adjacent waters for the first time with a drone strike, and the institutional response (Baltic Sentry becoming permanent, built on AUVs) confirms maritime autonomy is now standing infrastructure, not experimentation.

Money angles of the week:

- Patria demonstrated autonomous surface and aerial systems with the Finnish Defence Forces on Nov 5 and runs an unmanned-systems business line. Their software roles and the Defence Forces' ICT trainee postings are the realistic entry points for a C++/Python profile; applications this week should reference these events directly.
- Learn ROS 2 in two to three weeks (Nav2 basics) and say so in applications to Patria's unmanned unit. Almost every European defence autonomy effort, including Baltic Sentry's underwater robots, uses that toolchain, and very few junior Finnish developers can claim it.

## 3. Finland's border security

TL;DR: the Border Guard-led Sparta II salvage operation ended today as the burning ship was towed out of Finland's economic zone, after a response that included a suspected Russian airspace violation.

Top stories of the week:

- The Border Guard ended the Sparta II operation: the damaged ship was towed out of Finland's economic zone on Sunday Nov 22 by the Russian salvage vessel Murman, escorted by the Border Guard patrol vessel Uisko, the Estonian Navy's Kindral Kurvits and Alfons Håkans salvage tugs, with a Border Guard helicopter monitoring for pollution. No fuel or hazardous-substance leaks were observed ([Border Guard, Nov 22](https://raja.fi/-/pelastustoiminta-jatkuu-sparta-ii-aluksen-hinauksella), [Yle, Nov 22](https://yle.fi/a/74-20258284)).
- Full timeline from Nov 21: the strike hit at about 07:10 near Gogland island, the ship drifted into Finnish territorial waters off Loviisa at 12:27, the 16-person crew was evacuated to a Russian vessel, firefighting continued overnight, and a Russian helicopter is suspected of violating Finnish airspace by up to 4.1 km at 12:34. President Alexander Stubb said the reflections of Russia's war of aggression are now visible in Finland's near areas ([Yle, Nov 21-22](https://yle.fi/a/74-20258293), [Yle live coverage](https://yle.fi/a/74-20258284)).
- On the eastern land border, the government's position remains that the Finland-Russia land border crossing points are closed until further notice, per the June 4, 2026 decision; no new decision was made ([Finnish Government](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/en/-/1410869/finland-s-eastern-border-to-remain-closed-until-further-notice-2)).

Trend read of the week: Finland's border security this week was maritime, not the land border. A sanctioned, burning war-adjacent vessel drifting into Finnish waters plus a suspected airspace violation shows the Border Guard's operational tempo is now driven by Russia's war at sea.

Money angles of the week:

- Build a public dashboard that plots AIS vessel tracks in the Gulf of Finland and flags ships on open sanctions lists. The data is free, the geopolitical hook is this week's news, and it shows Finnish employers exactly the situation-awareness software mindset the Border Guard, Traficom and maritime authorities pay contractors to build.
- Make a calm, timeline-based Finnish-language YouTube explainer of the Sparta II incident using the published minute-by-minute data. Finnish-language technical explainers of domestic security events are scarce and this one will be searched for weeks; it feeds @vilppes and doubles as job-market proof of communication skills.

## 5. Robotics

TL;DR: ROS 2 Jazzy now runs natively on Apple Silicon Macs, and physical-AI companies raised $2.5B in November so far.

Top stories of the week:

- ROS 2 Jazzy now has a native, self-contained setup path for Apple Silicon Macs, including Gazebo simulation, with Nav2 reportedly up and running in under two minutes on macOS, a notable drop in the entry barrier for local robot development ([OpenTrends Embodied AI digest, Nov 21](https://opentrends.io/trends/embodied/2026-11-21)). Research highlights the same day include a new agent framework for long-horizon robot planning with spatial control, and MAWAM, a joint world model for multi-agent manipulation ([OpenTrends, Nov 21](https://opentrends.io/trends/embodied/2026-11-21)).
- Physical-AI and humanoid companies have raised $2.5 billion across 26 rounds in November so far, up about 250 percent versus the same point in October (report updated Nov 22, rounds from earlier in November). Top deals: Genesis AI's $500 million Series A at a $3.5 billion valuation, Germany's Agile Robots raising a $500 million growth round (making Europe 24 percent of the month's capital), and Agility Robotics closing a roughly $465 million SPAC ([HumanoidMegaHub, Nov 22](https://www.humanoidmegahub.com/reports/funding/2026-11)).
- Earlier this week, Hyundai took full ownership of Boston Dynamics ([RobotToday, Nov 16-20](https://robottoday.com/article/robottoday-weekly-november-16-20-2026)).

Trend read of the week: the robotics toolchain keeps getting cheaper to enter (native macOS ROS 2) while capital keeps consolidating (Hyundai fully owning Boston Dynamics, $2.5B into physical AI in one month). The gap between hobby-accessible tooling and industrial-scale funding is where junior developers can credibly stand.

Money angles of the week:

- Record the ROS 2 Jazzy Nav2-in-two-minutes Apple Silicon setup as a short YouTube video and publish the exact steps and a flake to GitHub. Learning ROS 2 this month positions the same profile for Patria unmanned-systems and European defence-robotics roles suggested in section 2, since the toolchain overlaps almost completely.
- Agile Robots' $500 million German round confirms European industrial robotics is where the money flows. A portfolio repo driving a simulated mobile robot through a warehouse pick loop (ROS 2 + Gazebo, Python) is the fastest credible proof for warehouse-automation vendors around Helsinki and Vantaa.
