## 1. AI agents

Signal disclosed on Nov 20 that it is restricting the new AI agents built into mobile operating systems - Gemini Intelligence on Android and Onscreen Awareness in Siri on iOS 27 - from capturing Signal chats, because on-device helpers scan what appears on screen. Meredith Whittaker, Signal's president, called OS-level agentic AI an existential threat to privacy apps and admits the screenshot-blocking fix is incomplete, since agents can also pull data through the filesystem ([Signal blog](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/), [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/signal-blocks-apple-google-ai-screenshots/)). The ongoing fallout was still a top story across today's AI coverage ([AIBriefs, Nov 22](https://aibriefs.news/ai-news-today)).

Amazon's threat intelligence team says autonomous Cairn hacking agents, linked to China, found a zero-day in MONAI Label, a medical-imaging annotation tool. The agents reportedly went from mapping the API to writing files on a university server in about 11 minutes and planted a Python reverse shell for root. The path-traversal bug affects MONAI Label 0.8.5 and earlier, where authentication is off by default; a fix is merged but no patched release exists yet ([AIBriefs, Nov 22](https://aibriefs.news/ai-news-today)).

METR published an updated time-horizon study: the task length frontier models can complete keeps doubling, and models match or beat skilled humans on coding and optimization tasks, while lagging on open-ended judgment tasks ([AIBriefs, Nov 22](https://aibriefs.news/ai-news-today)).

On the platform side, AWS made two agent-infrastructure moves on Nov 21: AWS Quick now supports plugins that bundle agents, skills, connectors and flows into one installable unit, and Web Search on Amazon Bedrock and AgentCore gained new language and country-aware search options ([Changes.Watch changelog, Nov 21](https://www.changes.watch/updates/daily/2026-11-21)).

Wider context that closed this week (labeled Nov 16-22): Anthropic, the company behind the Claude agent stack, closed its Nasdaq IPO on Friday at roughly $93.5 billion gross plus about $26 billion in a private placement to AMD, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and others, trading as ANT since Nov 19 ([AI World Today, Nov 22](https://aiworldtoday.com/news/weekly-ai-news-anthropic-ipo-november-22-2026)).

## 2. Defence technology

The big story: drones struck the Sparta II, a US-sanctioned Russian cargo ship operated under the Russian Defence Ministry, on Saturday morning Nov 21 in the eastern Gulf of Finland while it sailed from St Petersburg to Kaliningrad. The ship lost steering, drifted, and burned for over a day. Finland's Defence Forces said Ukraine was probably behind the strike, noting Ukraine had claimed a drone hit on the Russian corvette Naro-Fominsk near Gogland island only days earlier ([Al Jazeera, Nov 21](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/21/russia-says-kaliningrad-bound-ship-struck-by-drones-in-gulf-of-finland), [DroneXL, Nov 22](https://dronexl.co/2026/11/22/sparta-ii-russian-military-cargo-ship-drone-strike-gulf-of-finland/)). Analysis of the strike bringing the war into Finnish-adjacent waters was published Nov 21 by [Defence Matters](https://defencematters.eu/ukrainian-drone-strike-sparta-ii-finland-baltic-sea/).

Nato's Baltic Sentry activity, set up in January 2025 to guard Baltic undersea cables and pipelines, is hardening into a permanent maritime mission built around autonomous underwater vehicles: German and Finnish naval units have published footage of Seafox remotely operated vehicles and HUGIN 1000 autonomous underwater vehicles inspecting seabed infrastructure, repurposing mine-countermeasure tech for cable protection ([Defence Matters, Nov 21](https://defencematters.eu/nato-underwater-robots-baltic-sea-cable-security/)).

In US procurement, the Air Force issued a roughly $16 billion engine-maintenance contract spread across dozens of companies, reported Nov 21 ([ArtificialWeapons daily brief](https://artificialweapons.com/daily/2026-11-21/)).

## 3. Finland's border security

The Border Guard has ended the Sparta II operation. The damaged ship was towed out of Finland's economic zone on Sunday Nov 22 by the Russian salvage vessel Murman, escorted by the Border Guard patrol vessel Uisko, the Estonian Navy's Kindral Kurvits and Alfons Håkans salvage tugs, with a Border Guard helicopter monitoring for pollution; no fuel or hazardous-substance leaks were observed, and the Border Guard's task ended once the ship left Finland's search-and-rescue responsibility area. Some containers remained warm on thermal cameras but no open fire was visible ([Border Guard, Nov 22](https://raja.fi/-/pelastustoiminta-jatkuu-sparta-ii-aluksen-hinauksella), [Yle, Nov 22](https://yle.fi/a/74-20258284)).

The full timeline from Saturday Nov 21: the strike hit at about 07:10 near Gogland island, the ship drifted into Finnish territorial waters off Loviisa at 12:27, the 16-person crew was evacuated to a Russian vessel, firefighting continued overnight, and a Russian helicopter is suspected of violating Finnish airspace by up to 4.1 km at 12:34. President Alexander Stubb said the reflections of Russia's war of aggression are now visible in Finland's near areas and praised the authorities' work. The Defence Forces believe the drones were likely Ukrainian ([Yle, Nov 21-22](https://yle.fi/a/74-20258293), [Yle live coverage](https://yle.fi/a/74-20258284)).

On eastern land border policy, no new decision was made in the past 24 hours; the government's position remains that the border crossing points on the Finland-Russia land border are closed until further notice, per the June 4, 2026 government decision ([Finnish Government](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/en/-/1410869/finland-s-eastern-border-to-remain-closed-until-further-notice-2)).
